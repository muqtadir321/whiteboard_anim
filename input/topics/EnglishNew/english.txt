It’s officially the strongest buyer’s market in America on record. So why aren’t home prices falling?

In September, there were about one and a half million home sellers in the U.S., but only about nine hundred thirty thousand buyers. That’s sixty-three percent more sellers than buyers. According to Redfin, it’s the biggest gap since they started tracking it in 2013.

Basic economics says too many sellers should push prices down.

But they didn’t. The typical existing home sold for about four hundred seventeen thousand dollars in September. That’s higher than a year ago, for the thirty-ninth month in a row, according to the National Association of Realtors.

So what’s going on?

Here’s the secret. Almost four out of ten American homeowners have no mortgage at all. No monthly payment. No pressure. No reason to sell cheap.

So when the offers are too low, they simply say no, and take the house off the market. In December, a record number of sellers pulled their listings. And in January, nearly forty-five thousand of those homes came right back, the most for any January since 2016.

It’s like a shop that would rather close its doors for a few months than ever put a sale sign in the window.

So if you’re waiting for prices to crash, don’t hold your breath. In this market, the real power isn’t a lower price. It’s negotiation. Ask for repairs, closing costs, or a rate buydown.

Sellers aren’t desperate. They’re just patient.
