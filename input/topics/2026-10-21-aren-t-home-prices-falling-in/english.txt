[news] Why home prices aren’t falling in the US despite a growing surplus of sellers.

[article 1 "The 4% home loan is back—and for buyers, it looks like a gift."] Despite a growing number of sellers, home prices aren’t falling because of low mortgage rates. In August, nearly 1 in 7 new-construction listings offered a reduced rate, making them more attractive. [chart MORTGAGE30US DATE] For instance, the 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 6.67% in August, compared to 4% for some listings. [article 2 "One of the biggest obstacles keeping the market frozen is mortgage rates."] Even with more sellers, higher mortgage rates are making it harder for buyers. The typical new home buyer’s monthly payment has increased by 5% in 2026. [chart MSPUS DATE] Meanwhile, the median sales price of existing [icon man] homes has remained high, hitting $417,500 in September, the 39th consecutive month of annual price increases. [takeaway] So, while there are more sellers, the market is being held up by high mortgage rates and stubbornly high prices.
